# Rumi Darwaza

## Narration
This is the Rumi Darwaza, the great gateway that has become the symbol of Lucknow. It was built in 1784 by Nawab Asaf-ud-Daula, the same ruler who built the Bara Imambara. Standing about sixty feet tall, its grand arch and delicate ornamentation made it one of the city's proudest landmarks. It is often said to resemble a gateway in Istanbul, and that is where the name Rumi comes from. Stand beneath it, look up, and imagine the Nawab's processions passing through.

## Facts
- Built in 1784 by Nawab Asaf-ud-Daula, also part of the famine-relief building works.
- Height is about 60 feet (roughly 18 metres).
- It is also called the Turkish Gate; its design is often said to be inspired by a gateway in Istanbul (Constantinople).
- It stands near the Bara Imambara and acts as a grand entrance to the old city.
- It is a symbol of Lucknow and appears on city branding.
- Visiting tip: it is beautiful when lit up in the evening, and photographs well at sunset.
