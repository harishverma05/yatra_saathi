# Bara Imambara

## Narration
Welcome to the Bara Imambara, one of the grandest monuments of Lucknow. It was commissioned by Nawab Asaf-ud-Daula in 1784, during a terrible famine. The Nawab began this huge project so that people could earn wages and food, and it is said that even nobles worked at night so the poor could keep their dignity. Inside is a vast central hall with a giant arched roof held up without beams or pillars. Above it lies the famous Bhul Bhulaiya, a maze of narrow passages. Walk carefully, and you may need a guide to find your way out!

## Facts
- Built by Nawab Asaf-ud-Daula, construction began in 1784 and continued for roughly a decade.
- It was a famine-relief project: it gave employment to thousands of people during a famine.
- The central hall has a vaulted roof with no beams or pillars, among the largest such arched halls in the world.
- The Bhul Bhulaiya is the labyrinth on the upper levels, with hundreds of interconnected narrow passages and many identical doorways. Local guides commonly quote over 1,000 passages.
- The architect is traditionally named as Kifayatullah, from Delhi.
- Visiting tip: go in the morning to avoid heat and crowds, and hire a registered guide for the Bhul Bhulaiya.
- It is a short distance from Rumi Darwaza, so the two are easily visited together.
